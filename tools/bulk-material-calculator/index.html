How this works

Volume is length times width times depth, with depth converted from inches to feet, divided by 27 to give cubic yards. Weight is volume times a typical density for the material: about 2,800 lb per cubic yard for gravel, 2,700 for sand, 2,200 for topsoil, 2,000 for fill dirt, 2,500 for broken concrete, 1,400 for compost, 700 for bark mulch and 550 for wood chips.

The number of trips is set by whichever runs out first, bed volume or payload. For stone, sand and soil it is nearly always payload: a level short bed of gravel weighs about two tons, far beyond what a half-ton pickup may carry. The gauge shows how a level bed of your material compares with your payload, and the warning tells you what share of the bed to ask the yard to fill.

Questions people ask

How much gravel can a half-ton pickup carry?
Typically about half a cubic yard, which looks like very little in the bed. Mulch is the opposite: a full, heaped bed is usually within payload.
Why is my delivered weight higher than this?
Moisture. Rain-soaked topsoil or sand can weigh 20% or more above the dry figure. Leave a margin after wet weather.
Is delivery cheaper than hauling it myself?
Often, beyond two or three cubic yards. Compare the delivery fee with your trips multiplied by fuel and time.
