How this works

Cost per mile has two parts. Fixed costs are the bills that arrive whether the truck moves or not; divide the monthly total by the miles you actually drive. Variable costs rise with every mile: fuel (price per gallon divided by miles per gallon), maintenance and tires, and driver pay. Add them and you have the lowest rate at which a mile pays for itself.

The load check divides the offered rate by all miles, loaded plus deadhead, and subtracts your cost for those miles. A load paying $2.50 per loaded mile over 400 miles with 100 miles of deadhead really pays $2.00 across the 500 miles you drive.

The defaults are placeholders, not benchmarks. Use your own statements, and pay yourself in the driver line so that a profit is a profit and not just your wage.

Questions people ask

Why does driving more miles lower my cost per mile?
Fixed costs are spread over more miles. That only helps if the extra miles pay more than your variable cost.
Should I count my own pay as a cost?
Yes. If the truck only breaks even after you work for free, the business is losing money.
What else should I set aside?
Taxes, a maintenance reserve for major repairs, and downtime. Many operators add a few cents per mile for each.
