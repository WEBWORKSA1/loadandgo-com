How this works

Density is weight divided by volume. The calculator multiplies length, width and height in inches, divides by 1,728 for cubic feet, then divides the weight by that volume. The class is read from the standard density scale that less-than-truckload carriers use:

The scale grew from 11 steps to 13 in July 2025, when classes 55 and 50 were added for the densest freight and many commodities moved to density-based classing. Lower class numbers cost less per hundred pounds. Example: a 48 by 40 by 48 inch pallet weighing 500 lb is 53.3 cubic feet and 9.4 lb per cubic foot, class 100.

Questions people ask

Is the result my official class?
It is an estimate from density alone. Some commodities are classed on handling, stowability or liability instead. The National Motor Freight Classification is the authority, and your carrier or broker can confirm the item number.
How can I lower my class?
Raise density. Remove empty space in cartons, avoid overhang, and build pallets tight and square. A shorter pallet with the same weight moves up the density scale.
What if the carrier measures something different?
The carrier's inspection wins and you are billed the difference, often with a fee. Measure the widest points and weigh the finished pallet.
