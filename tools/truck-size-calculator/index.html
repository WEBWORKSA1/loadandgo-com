How this works

A fully furnished room takes roughly 150 to 200 cubic feet of truck space once the furniture and its boxes are stacked. The calculator multiplies your room count by the figure you pick, adds 35 cubic feet for each large appliance, 3 cubic feet for each extra medium box, and an allowance for garage and patio items.

Nobody loads a truck perfectly, so the total is divided by a loading factor: 1.0 for tight professional stacking, 0.9 for a careful first-timer, 0.8 if you have many odd shapes. The result is matched to the smallest common rental size that holds it: cargo van (about 245 cu ft), 10 ft (400), 12 ft (450), 15 to 16 ft (760), 20 ft (1,000), 22 ft (1,200) and 26 ft (1,650).

Weight is estimated at 7 pounds per cubic foot of household goods, the rule of thumb long-distance movers use for planning. Example: 5 average rooms and 3 appliances come to 980 cu ft of goods, 1,089 cu ft of truck space at average stacking, so a 22 ft truck at 91% full. That is tight enough that the 26 ft truck is the safer booking.

Questions people ask

Should I size up if I am between two trucks?
Yes, when the gauge is above about 90%. A second trip or a last-minute trailer costs more than the price gap between two truck sizes, and a slightly empty truck is easy to brace with blankets and straps.
Do bedrooms alone tell me the truck size?
Not well. A one-bedroom home with an office, a full dining room and a packed garage can outgrow a truck sized for two bedrooms. Count every furnished room.
Can a rental truck carry the weight?
Usually, for household goods. Dense loads such as books, tools, gym plates or stone are the exception. Compare the estimated weight with the payload printed on the truck door or the rental agreement.
