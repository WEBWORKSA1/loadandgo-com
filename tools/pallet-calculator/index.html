How this works

The floor count tries the pallets two ways: loaded straight, with the long side running the length of the trailer, and turned sideways. It keeps the better result. For standard 48 by 40 inch pallets in a 53 ft dry van that is 26 straight or 30 turned, the figures dispatchers quote from memory.

If pallets can be double stacked and you give a loaded height, a second tier is added when it fits under the roof. The count is then capped by weight: the payload limit divided by the weight of one pallet. The ticket tells you which limit binds. Dense freight weighs out with floor space to spare; light freight cubes out long before the scale complains.

Inside dimensions are typical for each equipment type. Payload defaults are conservative road figures, so change the limit to match the truck you have booked.

Questions people ask

Why does a 53 ft trailer rarely carry more than about 45,000 lb?
The 80,000 lb gross limit on U.S. interstates covers tractor, trailer and cargo together. An empty rig weighs roughly 33,000 to 36,000 lb.
Can I always turn pallets sideways?
Only if the forklift can enter from the 48 inch side, and the freight has no overhang. Ask the shipper and receiver before planning on 30.
