How this works

Three numbers can each cap what you tow. The first is the gross combined weight rating minus the actual loaded weight of the tow vehicle: the more you put in the truck, the less trailer it can pull. The second is the maker's tow rating. The third is the hitch, which is only as strong as its lowest-rated piece. The calculator takes the smallest of the three and compares your loaded trailer against it.

It also shows a target for tongue weight: 10 to 15% of trailer weight for a bumper-pull trailer and 15 to 25% for a fifth wheel or gooseneck. Too little weight on the hitch is the classic cause of trailer sway. Too much overloads the rear axle and lifts weight off the steering wheels.

Questions people ask

Where do I find GCWR?
In the towing section of the owner's manual or the maker's towing guide for your model year, engine and axle ratio. It is usually not on the door sticker.
How do I weigh my setup?
A public truck scale weighs the tow vehicle and trailer for a small fee. Weigh the truck alone, then the truck and trailer together, and subtract.
Does a weight-distribution hitch raise my tow rating?
No. It spreads tongue weight across the axles and improves control, but the vehicle's ratings stay the same.
