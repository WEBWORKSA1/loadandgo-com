Landscape yards sell by the cubic yard. Trucks are limited by the pound. The gap between those two units is how a half-ton pickup ends up sitting on its bump stops with the bed only half full.

## Work out how much you need

Multiply the length and width of the area in feet by the depth in feet, then divide by 27. Depth is usually given in inches, so divide it by 12 first. A bed 20 ft by 10 ft, covered 3 inches deep, is 20 x 10 x 0.25 = 50 cubic feet, or about 1.9 cubic yards. Add 5 to 10% for settling and uneven ground.

Common depths: 2 to 3 inches for mulch, 3 to 4 inches for a gravel path, 4 to 6 inches for a gravel driveway base.

## What a cubic yard weighs

| Material | Typical weight per cubic yard |
|---|---|
| Gravel or crushed stone | 2,800 lb |
| Sand | 2,700 lb |
| Broken concrete | 2,500 lb |
| Topsoil | 2,200 lb |
| Fill dirt | 2,000 lb |
| Compost | 1,400 lb |
| Bark mulch | 700 lb |
| Wood chips | 550 lb |

These are typical figures for dry to damp material. Water adds a great deal: soaked soil or sand can run 20% heavier or more.

## What your truck can take

A pickup bed filled level holds roughly 1.5 cubic yards for a short bed and 2.5 for an eight-foot bed. Now compare weights with payload. Many half-ton pickups have 1,400 to 1,800 lb of payload once the driver is aboard. That is about half a cubic yard of gravel, a layer only a few inches deep. A level short bed of gravel weighs around two tons.

Mulch is the reverse. A heaped bed of bark is normally well inside payload, and volume is the limit.

Find your own figure on the door-jamb label and subtract the people in the cab. That, divided by the weight per yard, is the most you can carry per trip.

## At the yard

- Tell the loader operator the amount you want by volume, and say you are limited by weight. A loader bucket is often half a yard or a full yard; ask which.
- Have the material placed toward the front of the bed, over or just ahead of the rear axle, and spread it evenly side to side.
- Line the bed with a tarp. It protects the paint and makes unloading far quicker.
- Cover the load. An uncovered load that sheds stones is illegal in most places and breaks windscreens.

## On the road

A loaded truck needs much more distance to stop and leans more in corners. Leave space, brake early and check tire pressures against the door label before you set off.

## When to have it delivered

Count the trips. Beyond two or three cubic yards of stone or soil, a delivery fee is usually cheaper than your fuel and time, and it saves the wear on your truck. A small dump trailer hired for the day is another option, provided the trailer's weight plus the load stays inside your tow rating.
