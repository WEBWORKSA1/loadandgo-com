There are three ordinary ways to move a household, and they differ mainly in who does the loading and who does the driving. Price follows from that.

## Rental truck: you load, you drive

You collect a truck, load it, drive it, unload it and return it. It is the cheapest option for a local move and gives you full control of timing.

What the headline price leaves out: fuel, which is significant in a vehicle that returns 6 to 12 miles per gallon; damage cover, since your car insurance may not extend to a large truck; equipment such as pads and a hand truck; and for long moves, lodging and the days of your own time. One-way rentals are priced by route and season and can cost several times a local rental.

## Moving container: you load, they drive

A company drops a container or trailer at your home. You load it over a few days, they haul it and you unload at the other end. You avoid driving a truck and can pack at your own pace, and storage between homes is easy to add.

Check before booking: whether a container may sit on your street or in your complex and for how long; what size you really need, because a second container changes the price sharply; and what cover is included for the contents. Containers suit long-distance moves and moves with a gap between leaving and arriving.

## Full-service movers: they load, they drive

A crew wraps, loads, drives and unloads, and will pack for an extra fee. It costs the most and takes the least out of you. For a long move, a household's goods often share a trailer with others, so delivery comes as a window of days, not an appointment.

Price depends on weight or volume, distance, and access at both ends. Stairs, long carries and shuttle trucks for narrow streets are charged as extras, so describe both addresses fully when asking for an estimate.

## Comparing fairly

Build the full do-it-yourself figure first: truck, fuel, cover, supplies, paid help and travel. Then set it beside written quotes for the other two. Three things often tip the decision:

- **Distance.** The further you go, the smaller the saving from driving yourself.
- **Your time and your back.** A DIY long-distance move can take a week of leave. Put a value on it.
- **Risk.** If you load it, damage in transit is mostly yours to bear. Ask each provider exactly what its cover pays per pound or per item.

## A middle path

Many people mix the options: rent the truck and hire labor by the hour to load and unload, or use a container and pay a crew for the heavy pieces only. Paid loading help for a few hours often costs less than the damage and delay it prevents.
