Rental companies size trucks by bedrooms because it is easy to say. It is also the reason so many people end up making a second trip. Two homes with the same number of bedrooms can differ by a full truck size once you count the office, the dining room and the garage.

## Think in furnished rooms and cubic feet

A truck sells you volume, so measure your home in volume. A fully furnished room, with its furniture and the boxes that come out of it, takes roughly 150 to 200 cubic feet of truck space. Count every room with furniture in it: bedrooms, living room, dining room, office, a furnished basement. Leave out bathrooms and halls.

Then add the things that do not live in a room. Large appliances take around 35 cubic feet each. A garage with a mower, bikes and shelving can add 100 to 250.

## Typical truck sizes

| Truck | Typical cargo volume | Usually suits |
|---|---|---|
| Cargo van | 245 cu ft | A dorm room or a few large items |
| 10 ft | 400 cu ft | Studio or small one-bedroom |
| 12 ft | 450 cu ft | One-bedroom |
| 15 to 16 ft | 760 cu ft | Two bedrooms |
| 20 ft | 1,000 cu ft | Two to three bedrooms |
| 22 ft | 1,200 cu ft | Three bedrooms |
| 26 ft | 1,650 cu ft | Four bedrooms or more |

These are typical figures for each class. The exact cubic feet for the truck you are booking is on the rental company's specification page, and it is worth the minute it takes to check.

## Allow for how you load

Professional crews fill a truck floor to ceiling with almost no air. Most people do not, and awkward shapes such as bikes, lamps and exercise machines waste space around them. Divide your volume by 0.9 if you will stack carefully, or by 0.8 if you have a lot of odd items. That is the truck space you actually need.

## When to size up

If your load fills more than about 90% of a truck on paper, book the next size. The price gap between neighbouring sizes is small. The cost of running out of room is a second trip, a second day of rental, or a trailer hired in a hurry.

A truck that is a little too big is not a problem as long as you brace the load. Build tiers against the front wall, strap each one, and fill the gap at the back with soft items so nothing can slide.

## Check weight for dense loads

Household goods average about 7 pounds per cubic foot, which rental trucks carry comfortably. Books, tools, weights, tiles and stone are far denser. If a large share of your load is heavy, compare its weight with the payload figure on the truck door or rental agreement, and spread heavy boxes across the floor instead of stacking them in one spot.
