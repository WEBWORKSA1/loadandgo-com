Two numbers get quoted for every pickup: how much it can carry and how much it can pull. They are different ratings, set by different limits, and loading one uses up some of the other.

## The ratings

**Curb weight** is the truck itself, with fluids and a full tank, and nobody in it.

**GVWR**, gross vehicle weight rating, is the most the truck may weigh on its own wheels, loaded. It is set by the frame, suspension, axles, tires and brakes.

**Payload** is the difference: GVWR minus curb weight. It is everything you add, including the driver, passengers, cargo, accessories fitted after the factory, and the weight a trailer presses onto the hitch.

**Towing capacity** is the heaviest trailer the truck is rated to pull.

**GCWR**, gross combined weight rating, is the most the truck and trailer may weigh together.

**GAWR** is the limit for each axle. It matters when cargo sits behind the rear axle.

## Where to find them

Open the driver's door. The yellow and white tire and loading label gives payload for your exact truck in one line, as the combined weight of occupants and cargo that should never be exceeded. A second label nearby lists GVWR and the axle ratings. Tow rating and GCWR are in the owner's manual or the maker's towing guide, and depend on engine, axle ratio, cab and bed.

Use these labels and not the advertised figure. The headline payload for a model is usually the lightest, most basic version. Four-wheel drive, a crew cab and luxury trim can each take a few hundred pounds off.

## How the ratings interact

A trailer does not only pull on the truck. It also pushes down on the hitch, and that tongue weight, typically 10 to 15% of the trailer's weight, is carried by the truck. It counts against payload.

Take a truck with 1,600 lb of payload towing a 7,000 lb trailer. At 12% the tongue weight is 840 lb. Add two adults at 350 lb and a 60 lb hitch, and 350 lb of payload remains for everything in the cab and bed. The tow rating said 9,000 lb, but payload ran short first. This is the usual way half-ton trucks end up overloaded.

GCWR works the other way round. Every pound you put in the truck comes off the trailer you can pull.

## The lowest rating wins

A towing setup is a chain: truck, receiver, ball mount, ball, coupler, trailer, tires. Each has its own rating, stamped or labelled on it. Your limit is the smallest number in the chain.

## Weigh it once

The only way to know your real weights is a scale. Public truck scales weigh a vehicle and trailer for a small fee and give axle-by-axle figures. Do it once with your normal load and you can stop guessing.
