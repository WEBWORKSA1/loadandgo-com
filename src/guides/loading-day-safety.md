Strained backs and crushed fingers are the usual injuries of a do-it-yourself move, and they tend to come late in the day, when people are tired and rushing. A little equipment and a few rules prevent most of them.

## Equipment worth renting

- **Hand truck.** For stacks of boxes and for tall items tipped back onto its wheels.
- **Appliance dolly.** A hand truck with a strap and stair glides, made for fridges and washers.
- **Furniture dolly.** A flat board on four casters for dressers, cabinets and pianos on level floors.
- **Lifting straps.** Shoulder or forearm straps let two people carry heavy items with their legs instead of their hands.
- **Furniture pads and stretch wrap.** Protection for the item and for your walls.
- **Gloves and closed-toe shoes** with a firm grip.

## Lifting

Test the weight before committing. Stand close, feet apart, bend at the knees and hips, and keep the load against your body as you stand. Turn by moving your feet, not by twisting. If you cannot lift it comfortably to waist height, it is a two-person item or a job for a dolly.

For two-person carries, agree the route and the commands before lifting. One person leads and calls the steps, turns and set-down.

## Prepare the route

Measure large items against doorways and stairwells before moving them. Remove doors from hinges if a few centimetres will make the difference. Clear the path of rugs, cables and toys, prop doors open, and cover floors and corners that will take knocks. Outside, clear ice, wet leaves and loose gravel from the walkway.

## Ramps

Set the ramp on firm, level ground and make sure its hooks are fully seated on the truck. Keep it dry and clean.

Going up, push the dolly ahead of you. Coming down, go backwards with the dolly above you, so the load cannot run away, and never stand directly below a heavy item on a ramp. A spotter stands to the side, not underneath. One person on the ramp at a time with anything heavy.

## Stairs

The stronger person takes the lower position, where most of the weight is. Keep tall items upright and low. Rest on landings. With an appliance dolly, strap the item tight and take one step at a time with a helper steadying from above.

## Appliances

Disconnect and drain washers and fridges the day before. Tape doors and cords. Keep fridges and freezers upright, and let them stand for a few hours at the new home before switching on. Gas appliances should be disconnected and reconnected by a qualified fitter.

## Pace

Start early, drink water, eat properly and stop for breaks before you feel you need them. Keep children and pets in a closed room or with a friend. When people are tired and the light is going, stop and finish tomorrow. Nothing in the truck is worth an injury.
