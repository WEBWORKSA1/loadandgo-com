A well-loaded truck drives better, holds more and delivers furniture without scars. The method is simple and it is the same one crews use every day: heavy and square first, built in tiers, strapped as you go.

## Before the first item

Park on level ground with the ramp on a firm surface. Lay out pads, straps and the hand truck where you can reach them. Take apart bed frames and table legs, and tape each bag of hardware to its largest piece. Stage boxes near the door by weight: heavy, medium, light.

## Where the weight goes

Put the heaviest things low and at the front of the cargo box, against the wall behind the cab. Appliances, dressers, safes and book boxes belong here. Weight that sits low and forward keeps the truck steady under braking and stops it leaning in corners. Balance the load left to right as you go.

## Build tiers

A tier is one wall of cargo, the full width of the truck and as high as you can safely reach, about two to three feet deep.

1. Start with a base of heavy, square furniture and appliances.
2. Stack boxes on the base, heaviest at the bottom, lightest at the top.
3. Fill gaps with soft goods: bags of bedding, cushions, rugs.
4. Run a strap across the face of the finished tier to the tie rails on both walls.
5. Start the next tier.

Strapping every tier is the step people skip, and it is the one that stops a whole load sliding backwards the first time you climb a hill.

## Awkward items

- **Mattresses and box springs** go on edge along a side wall, in bags, strapped flat to the wall.
- **Sofas** can stand on end in a corner if the ceiling allows. Pad the arm that takes the weight.
- **Mirrors, glass and artwork** travel on edge, never flat, wedged between a mattress and a wall.
- **Tables** go top down on pads with the legs removed, and light boxes fill the space above.
- **Fridges and freezers** stay upright.

## What goes on last

The last tier holds what you need first: the first-night box, bedding, tools, the vacuum, cleaning supplies and anything for children or pets. Plants and items that must not be crushed also go at the back, on top.

## Close up and check

Fill the final gap between the load and the door so nothing can tip, or brace the last tier with a strap or a load bar. After the first ten miles, stop somewhere safe and look inside. Loads settle, and a strap that needs one more pull now saves a broken lamp later.
