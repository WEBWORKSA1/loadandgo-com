About and methods

LoadandGo.com exists to answer one family of questions: will it fit, is it too heavy, how do I hold it down, and what will it cost to get there.

Who we are

An independent online publisher. We are not a moving company, rental company, carrier, broker or equipment seller, and no company in those trades owns or controls the site.

Where the numbers come from

  • Cargo securement. The tie-down calculator follows the minimum number of tie-downs and the aggregate working load limit in the U.S. federal cargo securement rules, 49 CFR 393.106 and 393.110.
  • Freight class. The class estimate uses the standard 13-step density scale adopted by the National Motor Freight Classification in 2025.
  • Fuel prices. Weekly retail gasoline and diesel averages come from the U.S. Energy Information Administration and are refreshed automatically.
  • Vehicle ratings. Payload and towing calculators use the definitions vehicle and hitch makers publish: GVWR, GCWR, curb weight, tongue weight. Your own vehicle's stickers and manual supply the values.
  • Typical sizes. Truck, trailer and container volumes and material weights are typical figures for each class. The page says so wherever one is used.

How we built the site

Before writing a line, we read more than forty sites that people already use to plan loads: truck and container rental companies, van lines, moving marketplaces, load boards, freight platforms, load-planning software and large calculator libraries. Most of them answer a sizing or weight question only as a step toward a booking. We set out to answer the question fully and let the reader decide what to do next.

How pages stay current

An automated job refreshes fuel prices, headlines and videos several times a day and rebuilds every page. Guides carry a review date. When a rule changes, as freight classification did in 2025, we update the calculator and its explanation together.

How the site is funded

Advertising served by Google AdSense, labelled sponsorships, referral fees from providers we introduce to readers, and reader support. None of these changes a calculator result.

Corrections

If a result looks wrong or a rule has changed, tell us with the numbers you entered. We check every report and fix confirmed errors quickly.

Limits

Results are planning estimates. They are not engineering, legal or professional advice, and they do not replace the ratings on your equipment or the judgment of a qualified person looking at the actual load. See the disclaimer.
