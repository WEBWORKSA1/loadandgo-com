Glossary

The words printed on door stickers, strap tags and shipping paperwork, in plain language.

Aggregate working load limit
The working load limits of every tie-down on a piece of cargo added together. U.S. commercial rules require it to be at least half the cargo's weight.
Axle rating (GAWR)
The most weight one axle may carry. A vehicle can be under its total rating and still overload one axle if the cargo sits in the wrong place.
Bill of lading
The shipping document that acts as receipt, contract and description of the freight. Check piece count and condition before signing it at delivery.
Binding estimate
A mover's written price that cannot rise unless you add items or services. A non-binding estimate can change with the actual weight.
Bobtail
A tractor driving without a trailer.
Bulkhead
The front wall of a cargo area. Butting cargo against it stops forward movement.
Cube out
To run out of space before reaching the weight limit. Light, bulky freight cubes out.
Curb weight
A vehicle's weight with fluids and a full tank but no people or cargo.
Deadhead
Miles driven empty, usually to reach the next pickup. They cost money and earn none.
Density
Weight divided by volume, in pounds per cubic foot. It is the main driver of LTL freight class.
Dry van
An enclosed, unrefrigerated trailer. The 53 ft dry van is the standard U.S. truckload trailer.
Dunnage
Material used to fill gaps and brace cargo: lumber, air bags, cardboard, blankets.
E-track
Steel rails on a cargo wall or floor with slots for strap fittings and load bars.
Freight class
One of 18 classes from 50 to 500 that LTL carriers use to price freight. Lower classes are denser and cheaper to ship per pound.
FTL (full truckload)
A shipment that hires the whole trailer, moving straight from origin to destination.
GCWR
Gross combined weight rating: the most a tow vehicle, its trailer and everything in both may weigh together.
GVWR
Gross vehicle weight rating: the most a vehicle may weigh fully loaded, including people, fuel, cargo and tongue weight.
Hand truck
A two-wheeled upright dolly for boxes and appliances.
Household goods broker
A company that arranges a move and hands it to a motor carrier. In the U.S. a broker for interstate moves must be registered with the federal regulator.
Liftgate
A powered platform at the back of a truck that raises freight from the ground. Carriers charge for it on LTL deliveries without a dock.
Linear feet
The length of trailer floor a shipment occupies. Long shipments can be priced by it instead of by class.
Load bar
An adjustable bar that braces between cargo walls to keep freight from shifting.
LTL (less than truckload)
Freight that shares a trailer with other shippers' freight and is priced by weight, class and distance.
NMFC
The National Motor Freight Classification, the published standard that assigns freight classes to commodities.
Payload
The weight a vehicle can carry: GVWR minus curb weight. People, cargo and tongue weight all count.
Pin weight
The downward force a fifth-wheel or gooseneck trailer puts on the hitch in the truck bed.
Ratchet strap
A webbing tie-down tightened with a ratcheting buckle. Its working load limit is on the sewn-in tag.
Reefer
A refrigerated trailer.
Tare weight
The empty weight of a container, trailer or truck.
Tongue weight
The downward force a bumper-pull trailer puts on the hitch ball. The usual target is 10 to 15% of trailer weight.
Trailer sway
Side-to-side swinging of a trailer, most often caused by too little tongue weight or too much speed.
USDOT number
The identifier the U.S. Department of Transportation assigns to carriers and movers. Use it to look up registration and complaint records.
Weigh out
To reach the weight limit before running out of space. Dense freight weighs out.
Weight-distribution hitch
A hitch with spring bars that shifts some tongue weight to the front axle and the trailer axles. It improves control without raising any rating.
Working load limit (WLL)
The greatest load a strap, chain or anchor should carry in normal use, typically one third of its breaking strength.
